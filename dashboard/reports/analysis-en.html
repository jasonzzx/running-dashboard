In Road / outdoor, median HR changes by -1.5 bpm, while median moving pace changes by -9.3 seconds/km (positive means slower). The newer and older samples contain 7 and 2 runs. At least one sample is below five runs, so do not promote the difference into a trend.

Outdoor HR comparisons also depend on temperature, wind, elevation, stops and workout intent. A lower HR with a faster median pace is interesting, but the small or unmatched sample can still explain it.

For a useful follow-up, repeat a comfortable route or machine session with the same sensor and similar duration; record conditions and perceived effort. Look for a repeated pattern rather than treating one reading, a device threshold or activity-average pace as a validated training-zone test.

Priorities for the next training cycle

1. Recover, then establish a usable baseline

After the recorded marathon, allow recovery rather than setting the prior 28-day volume as an immediate target. Resume short easy running when normal movement is comfortable and symptoms are settling. This archive does not describe your present condition. UCLA’s recovery guidance supports time away from running and gradual return [1].

2. Preserve total volume while changing specificity

The recent road share is 57.0%. If the next goal is a road race, use a gradual substitution of road sessions for some treadmill work, and track total distance and running days together. The data support monitoring the redistribution; they do not prescribe a universal weekly mileage target.

3. Test durability with a small, repeatable task

Once easy training is stable, a possible coaching experiment is a controlled late segment in an occasional long road run, with an easy alternative the following week. Keep the rest of that week manageable. Compare the same route, effort, duration and fueling before expanding the segment; avoid increasing length and intensity simultaneously.

4. Make fueling observable

Log carbohydrate, fluid intake, timing and gut comfort during long sessions. AIS lists 30–60 g/hour for 1–2.5 hours and up to 90 g/hour for longer endurance events; individual tolerance requires practice [2]. The archive does not record actual intake and cannot identify a fueling deficit.

5. Track decisions rather than chase scores

Use three checks: repeatable easy-run effort, late-segment pace at comparable effort, and recovery across successive days. If these disagree, hold the progression and investigate conditions. No single wearable score or faster training average validates a marathon target.
